# Street-Connected Children — Kenya & Tanzania

## MawaHub focus
MawaHub recognizes children living or working on the streets as children facing intersecting vulnerabilities—not as a problem to be hidden. The platform's rehabilitation work is designed around safety, family/community assessment, education, health, psychosocial support, skills and long-term reintegration.

## Kenya
Street-connected children in Kenya can face poverty, family separation, abuse, neglect, unsafe housing, interrupted schooling, exploitation, substance exposure and stigma. Nairobi and other urban centres can be important areas for coordinated outreach, but the response should be community-specific and based on verified local needs.

### MawaHub response pathway
1. Safe, child-centred outreach and referral.
2. Immediate protection and safeguarding assessment.
3. Family tracing and case management where appropriate.
4. Education catch-up, school reintegration and learning support.
5. Psychosocial support and access to health services through qualified partners.
6. Skills and livelihood support for older adolescents, where age-appropriate.
7. Follow-up after reintegration to reduce the risk of returning to street life.

## Tanzania
In Tanzania, children living or working on the streets may experience poverty, family instability, disrupted education, exploitation, violence, poor access to healthcare and social stigma. Responses should work with local authorities, social welfare professionals, shelters, families and community organisations rather than relying on one-off rescue activities.

### MawaHub response pathway
1. Identify children at risk through trained community partners.
2. Provide immediate safeguarding and referral support.
3. Assess family and community circumstances before reintegration.
4. Support school access, alternative education and life skills.
5. Connect children and families to appropriate health and psychosocial services.
6. Build safe pathways into vocational skills and dignified livelihoods for older youth.
7. Monitor outcomes after reintegration.

## What MawaHub will not do
MawaHub will not publish identifiable information about children, expose children to publicity for fundraising, promise guaranteed outcomes, or present unverified statistics as facts. Any sponsorship or case-management system must follow applicable child-protection, privacy and safeguarding requirements.

## Partnership model
MawaHub seeks collaboration with government social welfare services, qualified child-protection organisations, shelters, schools, healthcare providers, community leaders, responsible businesses and donors in Kenya and Tanzania.

## Goal
**From streets to safety. From survival to education. From vulnerability to opportunity.**
